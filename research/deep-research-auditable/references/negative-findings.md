# Negative findings

A negative finding is a statement about a search, not about the world.

## Required form

"None found in the material searched", followed by exactly what was searched:

- the sources (titles, editions or databases) and the access level for each: full text, excerpt only, abstract only, review-mediated, catalogue entry only;
- the method (reading, whole-text string search, index search, database query) and the strings or stems used;
- the date and any known gaps (front matter excluded, partial file, OCR errors, language coverage).

Weak: "No ancient author linked X and Y."
Better: "No link between X and Y was found in the full English text of edition A (string search for `X`, `Y`, and three variants) or in the excerpts of B. Authors C and D were not searched."

## Rules

- Never claim silence about a whole corpus when only part was read. Name the unread parts.
- Substring counts are finding aids. They include false hits and miss paraphrases. Report them with that warning, not as validated counts.
- Where the surviving source is incomplete, an absence argument is invalid for the missing part. Say so.
- Report null results from replications or database queries as results, with their scope.
- Keep four things apart in every report: primary findings (what a source itself says), modern arguments (what later scholars say), unverified leads, and bounded negatives.

## Template sentences

- "Searched: ___ (access: ___). Method: ___. Result: none found in the material searched."
- "Not searched: ___. No claim is made about these."
- "Count of ___ hits for `___` is a string-match result, not a validated count of ___."

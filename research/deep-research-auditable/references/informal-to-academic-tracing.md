# Informal-to-academic tracing

Informal sources (blogs, posts, videos, forum threads, repositories) are valid primary evidence for what their creators said, built or experienced. They are not scholarly evidence for empirical or theoretical claims. Tracing connects them to scholarship without erasing where the idea appeared.

## For every P0 or P1 informal lead

1. Paraphrase the central idea in one sentence.
2. Pull out distinctive phrases, named concepts, cited authors, linked works and implied constructs.
3. Search for the earliest, strongest and most direct academic treatments, using both the informal wording and academic vocabulary.
4. Look for academic items that support, qualify and contradict the idea.
5. Record whether the informal source originated, popularised, applied or merely repeated it (`06_CLAIM_EVIDENCE.csv` relationship).
6. If nothing suitable is found, write that exactly: "no appropriate academic counterpart found in the sources searched", with the queries.
7. Keep the informal item visible in the chain. Never replace it with the scholarly item that came later.

## Outcomes

- Traced: academic items linked, relationship and direction recorded.
- Unresolved: still a lead, perspective or hypothesis. It may be useful, but cannot carry a load-bearing claim.

## Do not

- Turn an anecdote into prevalence or a comment thread into population evidence.
- Treat vote counts or views as quality evidence.
- Cite the informal item for a scholarly claim just because the idea sounds right.
